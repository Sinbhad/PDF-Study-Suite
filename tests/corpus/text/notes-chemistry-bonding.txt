CHEM 101 - wk 4 - bonding

Types of bonds
ionic bond - transfer of e- from metal to nonmetal
covalent bond - sharing of e- pairs between 2 nonmetals
metallic bond - sea of delocalised e- around + ions
polar covalent - unequal sharing bc diff in electronegativity
electronegativity = ability of an atom to attract bonding e-
- increases across a period
- decreases down a group
- F is highest (4.0)

diff in EN > 1.7 -> ionic
diff 0.4 to 1.7 -> polar covalent
diff < 0.4 -> nonpolar

Octet rule
atoms gain/lose/share e- to get 8 valence e-
exceptions:
- H and He only need 2
- B ok with 6
- period 3+ can have expanded octet (eg SF6, PCl5)

Lewis structures
valence electrons - e- in outermost shell
lone pair - pair of valence e- not shared
bonding pair = shared pair
formal charge = valence e- minus nonbonding e- minus 1/2 bonding e-
resonance - when more than 1 valid Lewis structure, real molecule is avg (eg O3, benzene)

VSEPR
VSEPR theory: e- pairs around central atom repel so get as far apart as poss
2 domains -> linear, 180
3 domains -> trigonal planar, 120
4 domains -> tetrahedral, 109.5
4 domains w/ 1 lone pair -> trigonal pyramidal (NH3) 107
4 domains w/ 2 lone pairs -> bent (H2O) 104.5
lone pairs repel more than bonding pairs so angles get smaller

Intermolecular forces (IMF)
weakest to strongest
London dispersion - temp dipoles, all molecules, bigger molecule = stronger
dipole-dipole - between polar molecules
hydrogen bond - H attached to N, O or F attracted to lone pair on another N/O/F
stronger IMF -> higher bp and mp
H bonding is why water bp is so high for its size

Bond energy
bond energy = energy needed to break 1 mol of a bond in gas phase
breaking bonds endothermic, making bonds exothermic
shorter bond -> stronger bond
triple > double > single for strength
bond order - no. of bonding pairs between 2 atoms

Hybridisation
sp3 - 4 domains
sp2 - 3 domains
sp - 2 domains
sigma bond: head on overlap, 1st bond between any 2 atoms
pi bond: sideways overlap of p orbitals, 2nd and 3rd bonds
double bond = 1 sigma + 1 pi
